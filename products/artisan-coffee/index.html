Slow mornings, honestly roasted.

Sussegado is a Goan-Portuguese word for a laid-back, unhurried ease — the way we roast, and the way we think you should drink your coffee.

We roast small batches of Indian specialty coffee three times a week in a converted cashew warehouse two minutes from Assagao's paddy fields. Every bag is dated, every lot is traceable to a single farm, and every cup tastes like the place it came from — Chikmagalur, Araku, Coorg, Wayanad, Baba Budangiri, and the Nilgiris.

How We Work

From red cherry to your cup

Sussegado means unhurried, but coffee waits for no one. Here's the four-step chain that gets a lot from a hillside farm to your kitchen counter in under ten days.

01

Source direct

We buy from 11 family farms and two tribal cooperatives across five states, paying 20–35% above the local mandi price and visiting each farm at least twice a season.

02

Cup and grade

Every incoming lot is cupped blind by our tasting panel in Assagao. Anything scoring below 82 on the SCA scale is rejected and returned to the farm-gate market.

03

Roast in small batches

We roast 12kg at a time on a refurbished drum roaster, tracking bean temperature every 10 seconds so each origin hits its own development curve, not a house profile.

04

Ship within 48 hours

Bags are flushed with nitrogen, dated with the roast day, and shipped the same week — no coffee sits in our warehouse for longer than a Goan sunset.

Our Story

Started in a beach shack, roasted with intent

Sussegado began in 2019 when two friends — one a barista from Bengaluru, the other a farmer's daughter from Chikmagalur — got tired of shipping the country's best coffee abroad while everyone at home drank instant. They started with a 2kg sample roaster on a rented rooftop in Anjuna and a single origin from her family's estate.

Five years on, we're a fourteen-person crew running a proper roastery in Assagao, but the instinct hasn't changed: buy honestly, roast transparently, and never let a bag sit around long enough to go stale. We still surf before the morning roast when the swell is right, because sussegado is as much a philosophy as it is a brand name.

Today we work with growers across Karnataka, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, and Tamil Nadu, and we publish the price we pay for every lot on the bag itself — no exceptions, no vague "ethically sourced" stickers.

Brew Guide

Four ways to brew a Sussegado bag

No fancy gear required. Pick the method you have on your counter and follow the ratio — everything else is patience.

Ratio: 1:16 (20g coffee to 320ml water, 94–96°C)

  1. Rinse your filter and preheat the dripper and mug.
  2. Add ground coffee (medium-fine, like coarse sand) and level the bed.
  3. Bloom with 60ml water for 30 seconds, swirling gently.
  4. Pour the remaining water in slow circles over 2:30–3:00 total.
  5. Let it drain fully, then serve immediately.

Ratio: 1:15 (30g coffee to 450ml water, 94°C)

  1. Add coarsely ground coffee to the preheated carafe.
  2. Pour in all the water at once and stir twice.
  3. Place the lid on with the plunger pulled up, and steep 4 minutes.
  4. Press down slowly and evenly over 20–30 seconds.
  5. Pour off immediately — don't let it sit on the grounds.

Ratio: Fill basket level, fresh cold water in base

  1. Fill the base with cold water up to the valve line.
  2. Fill the funnel basket with medium-fine coffee, level, no tamping.
  3. Screw the top on tight and place on medium-low heat.
  4. Remove from heat the moment coffee starts to sputter.
  5. Give it a swirl before pouring to even out the extraction.

Ratio: 1:8 concentrate (100g coffee to 800ml water, room temp)

  1. Coarsely grind your coffee and add to a jar or French press.
  2. Pour in room-temperature filtered water and stir once.
  3. Cover and steep in the fridge for 14–18 hours.
  4. Strain through a fine filter or cloth into a clean jar.
  5. Dilute 1:1 with water or milk over ice to serve.
